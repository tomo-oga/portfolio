Research

I'm a third-year Data Science and Mathematics student at Northeastern University and a machine learning researcher at Matterworks in Somerville, MA, where I work on foundation models for molecules.

Most of chemical space is unexplored, and most of the molecules that matter in biology have never been characterized. I'm interested in models that learn chemistry well enough to reason about molecules outside their training data, and in evaluation that tells us honestly when they can.

Critical Mass: Structure-Disjoint Splits Drift With the Libraries They Are Built On

Tomo Oga, Devesh Shah, Cailum M. K. Stienstra, Gabriel Asher, Antonio H. de O. Fonseca, Niall O'Connor, Michael Widrich

Molecular ML measures out-of-distribution performance with structure-disjoint splits: threshold a pairwise distance, then keep connected components together. These rules are fixed at the scale of the library they were designed on. Taking spectrum-to-structure prediction as a case study, we make the exact MCES-10 split computable on 227,690 molecules, nearly 8× the library it was set on. At this scale the similarity graph percolates: the holdout nearly vanishes, and what remains is systematically heavier. Naive large-library splits therefore conflate structural novelty with extrapolation to larger chemistry.
